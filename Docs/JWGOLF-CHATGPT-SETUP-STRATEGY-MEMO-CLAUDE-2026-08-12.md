## PART A — WHAT JAMES DOES WITH CHATGPT (easiest first)

1. **Reply to enquiries in 60 seconds.** Paste the enquiry in, say: "Reply as James, PGA pro in Leeds. Warm, direct, no waffle. Ask their handicap and biggest frustration, offer two lesson slots, sign off with my number." Speed of reply is the single biggest lever on conversion — most pros lose bookings to slowness, not price.

2. **Build a "saved instructions" profile once.** Get him to paste in: who he is, the One Clear Fix method, tone (plain, confident, no jargon), prices, locations. Everything after that comes out sounding like him. This is the 20-minute job that makes everything else work.

3. **Post-lesson follow-up template.** Same day: "One clear fix we worked on today was X. Drill: Y. Do it 10 minutes, three times before we next meet." Two things happen — the pupil gets results, and he's created a reason to rebook. Ask ChatGPT to turn his scrappy notes into that email.

4. **Chase list.** Once a week: paste in names of people who enquired but never booked, or who haven't been back in 8 weeks. Ask for short, non-pushy nudges. This is found money.

5. **Local SEO content.** One blog post a week answering what Leeds golfers actually type: "how to stop slicing", "golf lessons Leeds", "best time to start lessons". Ask for a 600-word post in his voice with the One Clear Fix framing. Google Business Profile: photos weekly, post weekly, and a saved script for asking every happy pupil for a review.

6. **Social without living on social.** One filming session a fortnight, phone on a tripod, 6–8 clips. Ask ChatGPT for reel scripts: "One Clear Fix — 30 seconds, one problem, one cause, one drill." Then it writes captions and repurposes each into Instagram, Facebook, X and a blog paragraph. He films; the tool does the admin.

7. **Money work.** Use it as a thinking partner on pricing and packages — but he sets prices, not the machine.

## PART B — THE STRATEGY

**Positioning.** "One Clear Fix" is genuinely good because it's a promise, not a slogan — it says *you will leave with one thing, not twenty*. Own it by making it a format he repeats until it's boring to him: every reel, every blog, every lesson summary is literally headed "Your one clear fix." Repetition is what makes a phrase yours. The resilience story is the emotional hook, but use it sparingly — once on the site, occasionally in longer content, and always pointed at the pupil: *"I rebuilt my swing from nothing. Yours isn't as broken as you think."*

**Revenue.** The trap is selling hours. Fix in this order:
- (a) **sell blocks, not singles** — 6 lessons at a small discount, paid upfront; instantly multiplies revenue per enquiry and fixes retention at the same time
- (b) **group sessions** — 4 people, one hour, four times the hourly rate
- (c) **a low-cost online video review product (£25–40)** — send me your swing, get a video back with one fix — no travel, no diary, scalable, reaches golfers outside Leeds
- (d) **a monthly membership** for keen pupils — one group session plus unlimited video reviews
- (e) **fitting revenue and referrals**, which he's uniquely placed for given the tour-pro history

**Funnel and leaks.** Awareness is not his problem — conversion and retention are. The two leaks that matter: enquiries that go unanswered for a day, and pupils who take one lesson and vanish. Fix both with the block-booking sell and the same-day follow-up. Everything else is decoration.

**Proof.** Handicap movement is the currency. Ask every pupil their starting handicap, record it, and publish the deltas: "18 to 12 in five months." That plus tour-pro credentials plus the magazine bylines is a stronger stack than 99% of coaches in Yorkshire.

**His weekly rhythm — 90 minutes total.**
- Monday: 20 min replying and chasing
- Wednesday: 30 min filming or drafting a post
- Friday: 20 min review requests and rebookings
- Fortnightly: 30 min content batch
If it's more than that he won't do it.
