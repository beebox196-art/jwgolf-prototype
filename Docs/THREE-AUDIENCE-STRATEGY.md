# JW Golf — Three-Audience Strategy: Team Input & Synthesis

**Date:** 28 May 2026  
**Participants:** Bee (coordinator), Gav (research), Mel (design)

---

## The Challenge

James's current site tries to serve everyone at once. Three distinct audiences need different messaging, proof points, and calls to action:

1. **Golfers wanting coaching** — "Fix my game" mindset, want PGA credibility and quick results
2. **Corporate clients** — "Make our event impressive" mindset, want professionalism and structure
3. **Wellbeing/resilience audience** — "This story moves me" mindset, want authenticity and depth

---

## Team Input

### Gav (Research)

**Recommendation: Option A — One site, three clear pathways**

Key points:
- **SEO compounds faster on one domain.** Backlinks, content, and signals aren't split. Corporate and resilience searches are lower-volume — they benefit from riding the authority of a coaching-focused main site.
- **Real-world precedent works.** Virgil Herring (PGA + corporate + TEDx speaker) runs everything on one site. Erika Larkin, Coach Carolin, and Troon North do the same — clear sections, not separate domains.
- **Audiences overlap more than they conflict.** Corporate clients want the resilience angle for events. Golfers respond to the story. Speaking work feeds both coaching and corporate. One site lets you cross-promote naturally.
- **Option C (three separate sites) is overkill** unless corporate becomes a separate legal entity. Option B adds unnecessary complexity without meaningful SEO or conversion gains.

### Mel (Design)

**Recommendation: Option B-lite — One master site, three strong pathways with "section identities"**

Key points:
- **Not three brands — one brand with three "lighting states".** Same logo, typography, layout system. Differentiated by colour temperature and imagery:
  - **Coaching:** Fresh greens, white, graphite, swing-sequence imagery, data/trackman details
  - **Corporate:** Deep green/navy, warm metallic accent, venue photography, structured packages
  - **Resilience:** Soft neutrals, sage, muted coral/amber, editorial photography, human story feel
- **Homepage should not ask users to decode a big menu.** Hero says something like *"Golf coaching, corporate golf days, and resilience through the game."* Then three large route panels (could be designed as "flags" on a scorecard/course map):
  1. **Fix my golf** — "One Clear Fix coaching for golfers who want measurable improvement."
  2. **Host a golf day** — "Corporate events, group coaching, and handicap assessments."
  3. **Hear James's story** — "Five brain surgeries, resilience, epilepsy, and mental health through golf."
- **Each audience gets a distinct journey:**
  - Golfers: coaching page → One Clear Fix → packages → testimonials → book
  - Corporate: corporate page → event formats → who it suits → credibility → enquiry + downloadable one-pager
  - Wellbeing: story page → emotional hook → journey → speaking formats → invite CTA
- **Separate domains only as a future move** if the resilience/speaking work becomes its own serious business line.

---

## Synthesis: Where We Agree

**Both Gav and Mel land on the same core recommendation:** One domain, three clear pathways. The difference is nuance — Gav says keep it simple (Option A), Mel says differentiate the sections visually (Option B-lite).

**The consensus:**
1. **One domain (jwgolf.co.uk)** — no separate sites
2. **Three distinct pathways** from the homepage — not one generic homepage
3. **James is the unifying brand**, not "golf lessons" — his story connects all three audiences
4. **Cross-promotion matters** — the audiences overlap more than they conflict
5. **Separate domains only if resilience/speaking becomes a standalone business** — not now

**The honest trade-off:**
- Option A is simpler to build and maintain but risks each pathway feeling a bit generic
- Option B-lite adds visual differentiation without fragmentation — more design work upfront but each audience feels "spoken to"
- The cost difference is minimal (same infrastructure, just more considered design)

**My recommendation: Go with Mel's Option B-lite.** The extra design work is modest, and the payoff in audience resonance is significant. Each pathway should *feel* different enough that a golfer, a corporate buyer, and a wellbeing seeker all think "this is for me" within 3 seconds of landing. That's hard to achieve with Option A's uniform treatment.
