---

**Blocker before anything else: the testimonials.** "Mark Thompson (18→6)", "Sarah Johnson (beginner)", "Tom Williams (tour pro)" read exactly like placeholder copy. If they came from the scrape they may be real; if they came from Q or a template, they are fabricated attributions on a real business's site and must come out today. Confirm provenance before launch. Same category: the Titleist/Nike conflict. Nike Golf exited hardware in 2016, so "first Nike Golf Academy in Europe" is a historical credential and "Titleist Ambassador" is the current one — write it that way and the conflict resolves itself. Do not publish either claim unverified by James.

**Ranked by impact:**

**1. Proof, not adjectives.** The single biggest gap between "another golf pro site" and premium is verifiable specificity. "Fitted 50+ European Tour pros" is a stronger sentence than the entire luxury design system. Build a Credentials strip that is dense and factual: PGA, Leeds & District Union Head Coach, Moor Allerton, Howley Hall, Belfry, Oulton Hall, Birmingham, Today's Golfer / National Club Golfer / Golfshake. Logos where licensing permits, text where not. Put an abbreviated version above the fold — most visitors will never reach the About page.

**2. Booking friction is where enquiries die.** A contact form is not a booking flow. Ranked: real scheduling (even a well-styled embed) > a form that pre-selects the lesson type the user clicked from > a form. Put the phone number and email in the header on every page, tap-to-call on mobile, and give each pricing card its own CTA that carries context. Also: state the venue. A Leeds pro's most common pre-enquiry question is "where do I actually turn up?" — location, parking, what to bring, what happens in the first hour.

**3. Sharpen "One Clear Fix" from slogan to method.** As a hook it's genuinely good — it names a real pain (contradictory advice, 20 swing thoughts) and promises relief. It's currently a headline; make it a described product. Show the mechanic: diagnose → one fix → a measurable marker → what you practise until next time. Three steps, named, on the homepage. That converts because it tells the reader exactly what they're buying. Bonus: it makes the price feel like a system, not an hour.

**4. Resilience story — one page, one placement, understated.** What sells is not the surgeries; it's what they produced: a coach who knows that under pressure people can only hold one thought, and who has personally rebuilt a golf swing from zero. Lead with the coaching consequence, let the biography support it. Give it its own section on About, plus at most one short line on the homepage, in his own voice, no aurora, no drama treatment — restraint reads as strength. Cut any framing that positions him as inspirational rather than expert. Golfers hire competence; the story is why the competence is unusual, not the offer itself.

**5. Segment the offer.** Beginners, improvers, low handicappers, and fitting clients want different pages. "Free custom fitting" is a strong lead magnet and is currently buried in a price list — give it its own block explaining why it's free and what he measures.

**Keep:** the dark/gold direction and typography, the positioning line, the bento layout. It's the right register. Trim the effects (aurora, glass, scroll line) by roughly a third — premium is spare.

**Need from us (asks):** confirmed pricing; testimonial provenance plus 3–5 real named ones with handicap movement; ambassador status confirmed; venue/location details; and photography — James coaching a real person outdoors, hands/impact detail, one strong portrait. Stock golf imagery will undo everything above.
